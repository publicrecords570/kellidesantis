Document summary

This document contains a 2025 Petition to Modify Custody concerning Kelli D. DeSantis in the Court of Common Pleas of Pike County, Pennsylvania. The father requests shared legal custody and primary physical custody of the parties’ minor children.

The petition cites an alleged May 1, 2025 second DUI arrest, concerns about the mother’s housing, and the father’s asserted ability to provide stability, consistency, and care for the children.

The statements in the petition are the filing party’s allegations and representations, rather than findings by the court. The file includes an unsigned scheduling order and does not establish the court’s final ruling on the requested custody modification.

Subsequent temporary sole physical custody order

Following the filing of this petition, the Pike County Court of Common Pleas entered a signed order on July 15, 2025, awarding the father temporary sole physical custody of the parties’ three children until further order of court.

The order was signed by President Judge Gregory H. Chelak in case 365-2021. It states that the court considered the defendant’s Petition for Special Relief and schedules a hearing on the plaintiff’s Petition for Special Relief for September 26, 2025, at 1:30 p.m.

The linked petition is titled “Petition to Modify Custody”; the signed order refers to petitions for special relief. The order records a temporary physical custody ruling as of July 15, 2025 and does not establish the current custody arrangement or a permanent custody determination.

Petition Averments — Paragraphs 6–10

The following is a partial transcription of the petition, covering numbered paragraphs 6–10 and subparagraphs 6(a)–6(o). Original wording, including apparent typographical errors, is retained; spacing and list formatting are adjusted for readability. Statements below are those of the filing party. Refer to the PDF for the complete document.

  1. The Order of Court should be modified because the Father is seeking shared legal custody and primary physical custody and avers the Order should be modified for the following reasons:
    1. Mother was arrested on May 1, 2025 for a second DUI, while on ARD from her first DUI.
    2. Mother does not have a home in the state of Pennsylvania and is saying with a neighbor.
    3. Father can provide a stable, loving and wholesome environment for the children.
    4. Father can provide for the children’s, social, emotional, intellectual and spiritual growth.
    5. Father provides a routine and consistency for the minor children.
    6. Father can provide for the emotional and physical well-being of the children.
    7. Father can perform all parental duties.
    8. Father can provide for the minor children’s education, family life and community.
    9. Father has the availability of extended family and friends to assist him.
    10. Father desires to keep the siblings together in the same household.
    11. It is believed and averred that Mother is attempting to turn the children against Father and is inappropriately speaking about the Father in front of the minor children.
    12. Father is more likely to tend to the daily physical development, educational and special needs of the minor children.
    13. Father does not use drugs or alcohol.
    14. Father does not have any mental health or physical ailments that would prevent him from caring for the minor children.
    15. Based upon all the Custody Factors in 23 Pa.C.S.A. § 5328, said factors shall weigh in heavy favor of Father.
  2. Father has not participated as a party or witness in any other capacity in any other litigation concerning the custody of the children in this or any other Court.
  3. Father has no information of a custody proceeding concerning the children pending in any other Court of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania or in any other state.
  4. Father does not know of a person not a party to the proceedings who has physical custody of the child or claims to have custody or visitation rights with respect to the child who is not part of this litigation.
  5. The best interest and permanent welfare of the minor child would be served by granting the Father primary physical custody.
